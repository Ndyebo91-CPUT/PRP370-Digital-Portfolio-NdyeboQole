## Reflection: STAR Technique (Improving Job Application Skills) 

**Situation:** As a student in the Diploma in Information and Communication Technology program at Cape Peninsula University of Technology, I felt overwhelmed by the competitive tech job market in October 2025. With numerous peers vying for limited ICT roles such as software developers and network administrators, I noticed my existing CV lacked the polish needed to stand out to potential employers. This realization came during a career planning session where peers shared rejection experiences due to poorly structured applications, prompting my need for guidance to enhance my job application skills.
Task: My goal was to learn effective CV writing techniques tailored to the ICT field, ensuring my application highlighted relevant skills and projects from my diploma coursework. This involved understanding formatting standards, content prioritization, and how to showcase technical proficiencies like programming or database management to appeal to recruiters in the industry.

**Action:** On October 15, 2025, at 10:00 AM SAST, I engaged fully in a one-hour career counseling session with my lecturer, Mr. P Inderlal, specifically requesting advice on CV optimization. I asked targeted questions about layout (e.g., one-page vs. two-page formats), content focus (e.g., emphasizing diploma projects like PRP370S), and technical skill presentation (e.g., listing tools like Git or Python). I took detailed notes, capturing best practices such as using action verbs, quantifying achievements, and aligning skills with job descriptions. Using these insights, I drafted a new CV that afternoon, integrating a section for my PRP370 digital portfolio project, technical skills acquired (e.g., HTML, CSS), and a professional summary. I also sought feedback from a classmate, refining the draft to ensure clarity and professionalism.

**Result:** The session and subsequent CV draft significantly boosted my confidence, transforming my application from a generic document to a structured, employer-focused tool. The new CV draft earned positive feedback during a peer review, with suggestions to add certifications (e.g., a planned CompTIA IT Fundamentals course). Feeling more prepared, I plan to tailor this CV for upcoming job applications in the next semester (starting January 2026), targeting internships at local tech firms like Dimension Data. This experience not only improved my application readiness but also motivated me to attend additional workshops on interview preparation
