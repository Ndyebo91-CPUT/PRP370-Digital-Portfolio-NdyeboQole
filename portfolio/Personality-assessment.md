##Reflection: STAR Technique (Personality Assessment for Work Readiness)

**Situation:** As part of my Diploma in Information and Communication Technology program at Cape Peninsula University of Technology, I recognized the importance of self-awareness in professional settings, particularly in ICT teams where collaboration and adaptability are key. To operate effectively in such environments and strengthen my job application skills, I sought to better understand my personality traits. This need arose during a work readiness training module in early 2025, where discussions highlighted how personality influences team dynamics, conflict resolution, and career fit in tech roles like software development or network management.
Task: The primary objective was to conduct a comprehensive personality assessment to identify my professional strengths, weaknesses, and preferences. This involved selecting a reliable tool, completing the test, analyzing the results, and applying insights to real-world scenarios such as resume tailoring and teamwork strategies. The goal was to gain clarity on how my traits could enhance my contributions in ICT projects while addressing potential areas for growth.
**Action:** On February 17, 2025, I actively participated in a dedicated workshop organized by the university's career services center. I chose the Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI) assessment, a widely recognized tool for professional development, and completed the online questionnaire, which took about 20 minutes. The results classified me as an ISTJ personality type, emphasizing my preference for structure, reliability, and detail-oriented work—traits common in ICT fields for tasks like coding and system debugging. Following the test, I documented my findings in detailed notes, including key descriptors like "practical," "organized," and "loyal," and cross-referenced them with ICT career examples. To deepen understanding, I engaged in a group discussion with five peers, sharing how my structured approach could lead projects efficiently but might require flexibility in agile methodologies. We role-played scenarios, such as adapting to sudden changes in a team sprint, and I incorporated feedback to refine my self-perception.
**Result:** The assessment significantly boosted my confidence in leveraging my methodical strengths, such as organizing project timelines and ensuring quality control in group assignments, leading to better performance in subsequent team simulations where I took on leadership roles. I also realized the need for greater adaptability, prompting me to enroll in an online course on agile practices and practice spontaneous problem-solving exercises. Practically, I updated my resume to highlight organizing skills with specific examples, like managing a semester-long database project, which helped me secure a mock interview score of 90% in a later training session. Overall, this experience fostered personal growth, making me more proactive in team settings and better prepared for ICT job applications, with plans to revisit the assessment annually for ongoing development.
